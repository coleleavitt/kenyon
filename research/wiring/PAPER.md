# Does the fly's real PN→KC wiring hash better than random? A preregistered test on FlyWire v783

*Draft, 2026-10-02. Code and data pipeline: `kenyon` (this repository), `repro/src/bin/wiring.rs`.
Preregistration: `research/wiring/PREREG.md`, committed (git `ace57ec`) before any wiring run.
Every number here is regenerated by `scripts/reproduce.sh`. Raw output:
`results/wiring.md`, `results/wiring_{pooled,right}.json`.*

## Abstract

FlyHash models the projection-neuron (PN) → Kenyon-cell (KC) map of *Drosophila* as a uniform
sparse random matrix. The measured connectome is not uniform. In FlyWire FAFB v783, 285 PNs
contact 4,723 KCs through 21,509 connections. The median KC receives 5 PN inputs, and PN
out-degree is strongly skewed (mean 75.5, median 60, max 516).

We used the real matrix as a FlyHash projection. We compared it, at identical sparsity, with
three preregistered nulls:

- uniform random wiring with the same KC in-degrees;
- a degree-preserving rewire that keeps both degree sequences;
- a synapse-weight shuffle.

Each null was drawn with 20 seeds. The tasks were generic similarity search (MNIST mapped onto
PN channels), odour similarity (Hallem & Carlson receptor responses mapped through glomeruli),
and novelty detection with the fly Bloom filter.

**The real wiring did not hash better than its degree-preserving null on any task.**

- **Primary test (generic inputs, both hemispheres).** Real wiring was slightly *worse*:
  mAP 0.485 vs 0.500. The difference was −0.016, 95% CI [−0.022, −0.009], d_z = −1.2. Most of this
  comes from the near-total ipsilateral segregation of the two mushroom bodies (98% of claws).
- **One hemisphere.** The gap shrinks to −0.006 [−0.010, −0.001], smaller than the null's seed
  spread.
- **The degree sequence matters more than the wiring beyond it.**
  - The skewed PN out-degree costs about 4–5 mAP points on generic inputs (D − U = −0.040 and
    −0.051).
  - It gains about 2 points on odours (+0.025 and +0.021). This is because the PNs driven by the
    receptors in the odour panel hold more than their share of claws.
- **Novelty.** No contrast was robust. The real-vs-rewired difference on odour novelty changed
  sign between hemispheres.

These results agree with recent null results for connectome-constrained models under
degree-preserving controls (Dhiman 2026; Suárez et al. 2024, conn2res). Topology beyond the
degree sequence gives the fly no measurable hashing advantage in our tests.

## 1. Background

- **FlyHash.** Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha (2017) cast the fly olfactory circuit as an LSH
  family. Its steps are mean normalisation, a sparse binary random expansion (each KC sums
  about 6 of 50 PNs), and winner-take-all (WTA) through APL inhibition. Their matrix is uniform
  random, following Caron et al. (2013), who found PN→KC convergence statistically random.
- **Structured sampling.** Later connectomes showed that sampling is structured. Some
  glomeruli, notably food-odour PNs, are over-represented (Zheng et al. 2022 [outside corpus]).
- **The fair control.** A fair test of "the real wiring is better" must compare against a null
  that keeps the degree sequences.
  - Dhiman (2026; `topological_sensitivity_connectome_nets_2026` p.9 Tab. 2) showed that a
    flyvis "connectome advantage" of Δloss 0.184 came from initialisation. Against
    degree-preserving nulls from a shared initialisation it was +0.0017.
  - conn2res (Suárez et al. 2024, p.9) found that the fly mesoscale connectome does not beat its
    rewired nulls on memory capacity (p = 0.11), while mammalian connectomes do.
- **The gap this note fills.** We found no benchmark-style test of PN→KC hashing against a
  degree-preserving null on FlyWire v783 (doc 31 survey; INFERRED from bounded searches).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Connectome

- **Source:** FlyWire FAFB v783 Codex exports (CC-BY 4.0; Dorkenwald et al. 2024; Schlegel et
  al. 2024).
- **Edges:** `connections.csv.gz` has one row per (pre, post, neuropil) and is already
  thresholded at ≥ 5 synapses.
- **Filters:** class `ALPN` → class `Kenyon_Cell`, all neuropils, both sides, no further
  threshold.
- **Edge weight:** the synapse count summed over neuropils.

| Graph | Pairs | PNs | KCs | KC fan-in median (p10, p90) | PN out-degree mean / median / max | Synapses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pooled (primary) | 21,509 | 285 | 4,723 | 5 (2, 6) | 75.5 / 60 / 516 | 316,862 |
| right hemisphere | 10,304 | 142 | 2,356 | 5 (2, 6) | 72.6 / 60 / 480 | 140,874 |

The pooled numbers re-derive doc 31's planning counts exactly. Of the 285 PNs, 231 are
uniglomerular (55 glomeruli) and 54 are multiglomerular. 98.0% of pooled PN→KC pairs are
ipsilateral.

### 2.2 Arms (20 seeds each; preregistered)

- **R-b / R-w:** real topology; binary, or with synapse-count weights.
- **U-b / U-w:** each KC keeps its in-degree, and its PNs are drawn uniformly without
  replacement. In U-w the real weights are randomly permuted onto the new edges.
- **D-b / D-w:** degree-preserving double-edge-swap rewire (10 × E accepted swaps, no
  multi-edges) that keeps every PN out-degree and KC in-degree. D-w permutes the real weights.
  Degree preservation is asserted in code for every draw.
- **S-w:** real topology with synapse weights permuted across edges.

### 2.3 Tasks

- **T1, generic similarity.**
  - Inputs: all 10,000 MNIST test images, in a seeded order.
  - Mapping: a seeded Gaussian map onto the PN channels (x_PN = Gx, G_ij ~ N(0, 1/784)), then
    per-vector mean-centring.
  - Why this mapping: pixels have no glomerular identity. An isotropic map makes channels
    exchangeable, so arm differences reflect the wiring, not an arbitrary pixel-to-PN choice.
  - Retrieval: 1,000 queries per seed. True neighbours are the Euclidean top 200 in PN space.
    The metric is AP@200 / 200.
  - Hash settings:
    - primary: biological k = 5% of KCs (236 pooled, 118 right), binary, ranked by overlap;
    - secondary: k = 16, valued tags, ranked by Euclidean distance.
- **T2, odour similarity.**
  - Inputs: Hallem & Carlson (2006) responses of 24 receptors to 110 odours (DoOR 2.0, CC BY-SA
    4.0, column `Hallem.2006.EN`), plus spontaneous rate.
  - Mapping: each receptor maps to its glomerulus (table in PREREG). Every uniglomerular PN of
    that glomerulus receives the rate. All other PNs receive 0. This drives 103 of 285 PNs
    (23 glomeruli).
  - Retrieval: true neighbours are the Euclidean top 5 in receptor space. The metric is AP@5.
  - Same two hash settings as T1.
  - T2 has no task randomness, so R arms are a single value.
- **T3, novelty.**
  - Method: fly Bloom filter (δ = ε = 0) with 10-fold CV. Predicted novelty is the mean filter
    weight of the item's k = 20 winners. Ground truth is the minimum Euclidean distance to the
    stored fold. The score is Pearson r, averaged over folds.
  - Data: 200 MNIST-PN items per seed, and the 110 odours.
  - k = 5, 10, 40 are also in `results/wiring.md`.

### 2.4 Statistics

- **Contrasts:** paired by seed, so both arms see the same task draw.
  - P1 (wiring beyond degrees): R-b − D-b. **The only confirmatory test is P1 on T1, primary
    hash, pooled graph.**
  - P2 (degree heterogeneity): D-b − U-b.
  - P3 (weights): R-w − S-w.
- **Reported for each contrast:**
  - the mean difference;
  - a 95% t-interval over the 20 seeds, and a 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap;
  - Cohen's d_z.
- **Decision rule:** an effect is "detected" if the t-CI excludes 0. It is "relevant" if,
  in addition, |difference| exceeds the null arm's SD across seeds.
- **Everything else is exploratory.** There is no multiplicity correction.

### 3.3 Reading the results against the hypotheses

- **H1 (P1 ≈ 0): supported, with a twist.**
  - On generic inputs the real wiring is not better than its degree-preserving null. In the
    pooled graph it is detectably worse.
  - **Why.** Real KCs draw 98% of their claws from their own side's PNs. A side-blind rewire
    lets each KC sample all 285 channels, which here are independent, so it mixes more input
    dimensions per KC. This is INFERRED from the 98% ipsilateral fraction and from the effect
    shrinking to about 1/3 in the single-hemisphere graph.
  - **Single hemisphere.** The remaining −0.006 is detectable but below the null's seed spread.
    It is a candidate effect of within-hemisphere structure, such as KC-subtype sampling biases.
    It is not a demonstrated one.
- **H2 (generic: U ≥ R): supported.** Uniform wiring beat the real wiring by 0.056 mAP (pooled)
  and 0.056 (right). Most of this is the degree sequence: P2 ≈ −0.04 to −0.05, d_z < −3.6. A
  few PNs with out-degree up to 516 over-weight a few input channels, which hurts when all
  channels matter equally.
- **H3 (odours: R/D > U): partly supported, and only through degrees.**
  - The degree-preserving null beat uniform by about 0.02 on odour retrieval in both
    hemispheres.
  - The real wiring did not beat its degree-preserving null: rank 19/20 pooled, 10/20 right.
  - **Why.** The 103 PNs driven by the Hallem panel hold 41% of all claws while being 36% of PNs
    (out-degree mean 85.8 vs 69.6 for the rest). Keeping the degrees keeps that extra coverage.
    Uniform wiring removes it.
- **Weights (P3).**
  - On generic inputs, the real synapse counts are slightly worse than shuffled counts (−0.015
    and −0.010).
  - On odours the sign differs between hemispheres and hash settings.
  - We do not read anything into P3.
- **Novelty.**
  - On MNIST-PN every contrast is non-significant (null SD ≈ 0.08 at n = 200 items).
  - On odours the P1 contrast is +0.065 in the pooled graph and −0.045 in the right
    hemisphere. An effect that changes sign between two halves of the same brain is not a
    wiring advantage.

**Conclusion (plain).** In these tests the fly's PN→KC wiring is no better a hash than a random
graph with the same degree sequence. The degree sequence itself, notably the skewed PN
out-degree, is what moves performance. It hurts generic inputs and helps the odours whose PNs
are over-represented. This agrees with Dhiman (2026) and conn2res (Suárez et al. 2024): once
degrees are controlled, a connectome-specific advantage is absent or tiny. Claims that a fly
algorithm works "because it is the fly's wiring" should be tested against a degree-preserving
null (doc 31 §0).

## 4. Limitations

- **Odour panel.** Only 23 of 55 glomeruli (103 of 285 PNs) receive input from the Hallem panel.
  The receptor→glomerulus table is INFERRED from Couto et al. 2005 and Fishilevich & Vosshall
  2005 (outside the corpus). Or33b is split across DM3 and DM5. A DoOR consensus matrix covering
  more glomeruli would test H3 better. Using it needs the DoOR R tooling, or a re-export.
- **The generic map is a choice.** An isotropic random map makes PN channels exchangeable by
  design. Natural odour statistics are not exchangeable, and wiring might be adapted to them.
  T2 covers only one small panel.
- **Model simplifications.**
  - Rate-based, linear KCs with hard WTA. There is no APL dynamics, no KC–KC coupling, and no
    spiking thresholds.
  - The ≥ 5-synapse threshold comes from FlyWire's export. Weaker connections are ignored.
  - Edge weights are synapse counts, not measured efficacies.
- **Statistics.**
  - Only P1/T1/pooled is confirmatory. 20 seeds give coarse ranks for the single-valued T2 real
    arm (resolution 1/21).
  - There is no multiplicity correction across the roughly 60 exploratory contrasts.
- **One brain.** One brain (FAFB, female) was used. Cross-brain replication (hemibrain, MCNS) is
  the obvious next step. Schlegel et al. 2024 report that weak edges replicate poorly.
- **Reproduction context.** The k = 16 valued-tag setting inherits the R1 finding that valued
  tags help FlyHash only at very short codes (`RESULTS.md`).
