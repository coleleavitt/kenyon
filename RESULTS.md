This file has two parts. **Part 1 (predeclared)** was committed *before* any reproduction run;

## Part 1: predeclared bars and protocols (written before running)

Tolerance: a published number is **reproduced** if ours is within ±2 points (mAP in %,
correlation and accuracy ×100). Off by 2–5 points is **partial**. Off by more than 5 points
after an honest attempt is a **failure**, and it is reported as one (doc 31, card A1).

### R1. FlyHash vs LSH, MNIST, k = 4

- **Published:** fly 44.8% vs LSH 16.0% mAP (Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha 2017, bioRxiv
  10.1101/180471, p.9; Science 358:793).
- **Bar:** fly in [42.8, 46.8], LSH in [14.0, 18.0].
- **Protocol** (paper p.7–9, with the DenseFly paper's description of the same protocol for
  what the preprint leaves out):
  - 10,000 MNIST images. We use the 10k-image test set (fixed), in a per-trial random order.
  - Pre-processing: subtract each vector's own mean. DenseFly p.4 says this is the only step.
  - Queries: 1,000 random items per trial. True neighbours: the top 2% (200) by Euclidean
    distance in input space, excluding the query.
  - Predicted neighbours: the top 200 by Euclidean distance between tags, excluding the query.
    The tag keeps the winners' values (p.7–8).
  - Fly: m = 10d = 7,840 KCs ("a 10d expansion", Fig. 3). Each KC sums 10% of the inputs
    (78 pixels; p.8, "sampling 10%"). WTA keeps the top k.
  - LSH: k dense N(0,1) projections, all kept.
  - 50 trials. Each trial draws new projections and new queries.
- **mAP definition.** The preprint cites refs 31–32 without a formula. Ties in ranking are
  broken by the per-trial random item order.
  - Primary: AP@200 = Σ_{i≤200, hit_i} P@i / 200 (ml_metrics `apk`).
  - Also reported: the same sum divided by the number of hits.
- **Ablations (no published numbers in the text; expected direction from Fig. 2):**
  - (a) dense Gaussian 10d expansion + WTA: expect ≈ fly (Fig. 2A says sparse ≈ dense).
  - (b) no WTA: the "random tag" of k fixed random KCs from the same 10d expansion. Expect
    ≈ LSH (p.9: "effectively the same as just doing k random projections").
  - (c) no expansion: k sparse binary projections, all kept. Expect ≈ LSH (Fig. 2A).
  - (d) binary fly tag (indices only, ranked by overlap), for reference.

### R2. Fly Bloom filter novelty, SIFT

- **Published:** correlation with true novelty 0.535 ± 0.03 (fly), vs 0.345 ± 0.03 (LSBF) and
  0.002 ± 0.02 (classical Bloom filter) (Dasgupta et al. 2018 PNAS, Results and Fig. 3C).
- **Bar:** fly in [0.515, 0.555].
- **Protocol** (from the paper's text; the SI Appendix was not available offline):
  - n = 5,000 SIFT descriptors (d = 128): a random 5,000 of the ANN_SIFT10K base set.
  - 10-fold cross-validation. Insert the 4,500 training items. For each test item compute:
    - ground truth f(x, S) = min Euclidean distance to the training set (Eq. 1);
    - predicted novelty = the mean filter weight over the item's k winners;
    - Pearson r between the two over the test fold.
  - Report mean ± sd over the 10 folds.
  - Filter size m = 30 · n_train = 135,000 KCs. δ = ε = 0 (the paper's benchmark setting).
  - k ∈ {5, 10, 15, …, 50}.
  - Projection: each KC samples 10% of the inputs (13 of 128). This is GUESSED (the SI is not
    available), using the FlyHash setting. Inputs are mean-centred.
- **Statistic compared with 0.535:** the paper does not say which k the number is for, so we
  predeclare two:
  - (i) the best fold-mean over k (generous);
  - (ii) the mean over the k sweep (conservative).

  Both are reported, along with the full curve.
- **Controls:**
  - classical Bloom filter: k independent uniform hashes of the exact item; expect ≈ 0;
  - LSBF: k sign-random-projection LSH functions, each into its own m/k cells
    (Kirsch & Mitzenmacher 2006 form). Bits per function b ∈ {4, 8, 12, 16}, best b reported.
    Expect < fly.
  - Fly ablations:
    - dense Gaussian expansion: expect ≈ fly;
    - random tag (no WTA): expect a large drop.
- **Secondary** (same paper, odors): 110 Hallem & Carlson 2006 odors × 24 ORNs (DoOR 2.0 column
  `Hallem.2006.EN`, plus the spontaneous rate), k = 40, m = 30n.
  - Published: fly 0.657 ± 0.06, LSBF 0.537 ± 0.08.
  - Bar: fly in [0.637, 0.677].

### R3. FlyModel, class-incremental MNIST-20

- **Published:** accuracy over the classes trained so far, after 5 tasks (10 classes): 0.86 ±
  0.0006 (Shen, Dasgupta & Navlakha 2021, arXiv:2107.07617, p.7).
  - The brief's "0.86 on 20-class MNIST" is this number. It is measured after 10 of the
    20 classes.
  - At the end of training (20 classes) the paper gives FlyModel 0.75 (p.8, "0.75 vs. 0.54").
  - It gives sparse vs dense coding 0.64 vs 0.07, each averaged over the two learning
    rules (p.7).
  - Average memory loss: 0.07 (p.7).
- **Bars:**
  - accuracy after 10 classes in [0.84, 0.88];
  - after 20 classes in [0.73, 0.77];
  - dense-coding ablation at 20 classes in [0.05, 0.09];
  - mean memory loss in [0.05, 0.09].
- **Protocol** (p.4–5, p.11–13):
  - Encoder: LeNet5 with batch norm, trained on KMNIST (Adam, lr 0.001, batch 64, 25 epochs),
    then frozen. Its 84-unit penultimate layer is the input for MNIST and Fashion-MNIST.
    Labels: digits 0–9, fashion 10–19.
  - Tasks (0,1), (2,3), …, (18,19). Within a task, all of one class, then all of the next.
    One pass.
  - Test after each task on the test images of all classes seen so far.
  - FlyModel settings:
    - m = 3,200 KCs, 10 inputs each;
    - WTA keeps l = m/20 = 160 positive units, then min-max normalisation;
    - β = 0.01, α = 0, weights capped to [0, 1].
  - 5 random Θ (seeds 0–4).
- **Ablations:**
  - dense coding (no WTA): expect a collapse to ≈ 0.07 at 20 classes;
  - perceptron v1 (classic): expect worse than FlyModel (Fig. 4A);
  - frozen-encoder control: FlyModel on raw 784-pixel inputs (m = 31,360, 78 inputs per KC,
    l = m/20). Reported for reference, no bar.
- **Known deviation risk:** our encoder is a re-trained LeNet5. The paper's trained weights are
  not available, so encoder quality can move every FlyModel number. We report the encoder's
  KMNIST test accuracy.

### Amendment A1 (2026-10-02, before the SIFT run): R2 protocol from the PNAS SI

The R2 protocol above was written without the SI Appendix. The SI was then retrieved from PMC
(`pnas.1814448115.sapp.pdf`). It specifies the settings that R2 had marked GUESSED. Only the
odour secondary had been run under the guessed protocol by then (fly r = 0.38 with fan-in 2 and
r = 0.59 with fan-in 6, k = 40). The SIFT primary had **not** been run. From now on R2 follows the
SI:

- Pre-processing: each item scaled to unit L2 norm, with no centring (SI l.100–101).
- Ground truth: min **cosine distance** to the stored set (SI l.101–103), not Euclidean.
- Fly projection: each KC sums **6** random inputs (SI l.88–93; Alg. S3).
- k ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40, 50}; m ∈ {10n, 30n} (SI l.107–108). m = 30n is primary, with n the number
  of stored items.
- Classical BF: k independent hashes mod m (Alg. S1).
- LSBF (Alg. S2): k Gaussian d × 5 projections of the unit-norm input; each is quantised by
  floor((s + 0)/1) and the integer vector is hashed mod m. This replaces the predeclared
  K&M-block LSBF with a b-sweep.
- Odours: the dataset values as given (`Hallem.2006.EN`). The SI does not mention adding the
  spontaneous rate. With the spontaneous rate added is reported as exploratory.
- The bars are unchanged.
- The guessed (pre-SI) protocol is still run and reported, as `*_prereg_guess`.

Reading:
- The **controls behave as the paper says**:
  - a sparse binary expansion matches a dense Gaussian one (Fig. 2A);
  - removing WTA (random tag), or removing the expansion, falls exactly to LSH (p.9: "effectively
    the same as just doing k random projections").
- The **headline gap reproduces** at k = 4: fly ≈ 2.4–3× LSH.
- **Absolute level depends on the mAP definition.** The predeclared AP/R gives 10.4 and 3.5,

  0.8 points).

  ranking punishes non-shared winners by their magnitude. INFERRED: the paper's Fig. 3 likely
  used a different ranking, or binary tags.

### Deviations (all)

1. **R1 mAP formula.** The preprint gives none. The predeclared AP/R and the alternative
   AP/hits are both reported. Neighbour ties are broken by a per-trial random item order.
2. **R1 data.** 10k = the MNIST test set (the paper says only "a subset of 10,000"). The ground
   truth is Euclidean on raw pixels; hashes use mean-centred inputs.
3. **R2.** The protocol changed to the SI before the SIFT run (amendment A1, committed).
   Remaining differences:
   - SIFT source (ANN_SIFT10K);
   - n_train for m;
   - 3 projection seeds;
   - the LSBF hash is our own mix function, standing in for "a traditional hash function";
   - the classical BF uses a splitmix hash, standing in for MD5/Murmur.
4. **R2 odours.** DoOR 2.0's `Hallem.2006.EN` column, not the original table. Without the
   spontaneous rate (SI is silent); with it, reported as exploratory.
5. **R3 encoder.** Retrained by us in Rust/candle, with our seeded init, not PyTorch defaults.
   The batch-norm placement (after every conv/fc except the output) is GUESSED, because the
   paper says only "with batch normalization". Multithreaded float summation makes the encoder
   bit-reproducible only on the same machine.
6. **R3 dense ablation.** Only dense+associative was run (the paper's 0.07 averages two models).
7. **Bars.** No bar was moved after seeing results. The R2 protocol change is documented above.

Reading:
- **Cost per hash.** The paper's "20× cheaper" (preprint p.8) is per projection. With a 10d
  expansion and 10% fan-in, a FlyHash *hash* does 611k additions, against 3,136
  multiply-adds for 4 LSH projections. It is about 130× slower here.
- **Storage.** With decay or recovery the fly Bloom filter needs real-valued cells (f32 + u64
  timestamps). In the paper's static setting the cells are exact bits, as a classical filter
  (18.75 KB at m = 150k); the projection matrix then dominates memory.
- **Open optimisation (not done):** a bit-packed static filter and u16 claw indices.
